1. Visual Style & Palette
The "Neon Hacker" Look: The interface utilizes a deep charcoal and obsidian base (#131313) to create a high-contrast environment. This is punctuated by a vibrant Neon Green (#00ff00) primary brand color used for emissive glows, active states, and technical outlines.
Tactile Textures: Surfaces often feature subtle "blueprint" grids, scanlines, or carbon-fiber textures, giving the digital workspace a physical, high-tech weight.
Accents: Occasional "high-volatility" gradients (Magenta to Yellow) are used in  modules to inject energy and urgency.
2. Layout Composition
Brutalist Architecture: The layout is structured around rigid, high-precision grids. Borders are often thick and visible, emphasizing a "built" or "engineered" feel rather than a soft consumer interface.
Three-Column Workspace: On desktop, the layout typically features:
Side Navigation: A docked left bar for core tool switching (Viewport, Node Graph, Outliner).
Main Viewport: A vast, central staging area with a neon-precision grid.
Properties Panel: A right-aligned inspector for fine-tuning coordinates, materials, and physics.
Logic-Driven UI: In the Node Graph, the layout becomes organic and "wired," with logic nodes connected by glowing bezier curves.
3. Typography
Primary Font: Anybody: A versatile, modern sans-serif that feels both technical and expressive. It's often used in all-caps or italicized states for a sense of speed and momentum.
Secondary Font: Space Grotesk / Roboto Mono: These are used for "system-level" information, command-line inputs, and terminal outputs to maintain the developer-centric aesthetic.
Hierarchy:
Headlines: Bold, uppercase, and often tracking-tight for maximum impact.
Labels: Monospaced and small, mimicking the metadata found in professional CAD or 3D software.
